### The Bottom Line in Plain Language                                                                                                                                                                                              
                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   
The model is driven by a simple hierarchy:                                                                                                                                                                                         
                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   
1. Age is the anchor. The probability of wiry pulse goes from ~20% at age 25 to ~85% at age 60, regardless of everything else. This is the strongest signal by a wide margin.                                                      
2. The pulse harmonic (h3) is the modifier. When it's present (h3LBD > 2), it pulls probability down across all ages. When it's absent (h3LBD near 0), it pushes probability up. This is the sensor equivalent of the taut "string"
   feel a clinician detects — the loss of the softer underlying wave.                                                                                                                                                              
3. Gender shifts the baseline. A woman with the same age and sensor readings as a man will have roughly 30 percentage points higher probability. About half of this is a real physiological difference, half is the dataset being  
   72% female.                                                                                                                                                                                                                     
4. Choppy pulse is a force multiplier. When a pulse is already borderline, the presence of choppy characteristics pushes it decisively into wiry territory (e.g., 40-year-old woman: 43% → 84%).                                   
5. Everything else is fine-tuning. RET L, red dots, height — they matter at the margins but won't flip a prediction.

### The practical recommendation I'd give a clinical team                                                                                                                                                                          
                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   
1. Use it as a screening tool, not a diagnostic one. A patient scoring <10% or >90% is very likely correct. A patient in the middle needs a real clinician's assessment.                                                           
2. Validate on your own patients first. Collect the same sensor measurements from 50–100 new patients, run the model, and compare to your clinicians' diagnoses. If the AUC holds above 0.85, you can trust it.                    
3. Don't use the probability as a decision threshold. The class-weight balancing means the 50% threshold doesn't correspond to "50% chance of wiry pulse." Use the extremes (≤10%, ≥90%) as flags, and treat everything else as    
   inconclusive.                                                                                                                                                                                                                   
4. Recalibrate if you change devices. If your tonometer or retinal camera is from a different manufacturer, collect a calibration set before using the model.                                                                      
5. Watch the gender bias. If your clinic sees a higher proportion of male patients than this dataset (28%), the model will underperform for them. Consider a gender-stratified validation.
